# Working on Psychedelic Mandelbrot

- Owner: Jakes. He gives taste notes; Claude does the build. Keep replies short: what changed, how to check it, what you need from him.
- No build step and no dependencies. `index.html` must keep working when opened from any static host.
- Sets, places and text live in `data/sets.json`, never hard-coded in the page. Run `npm test` before every commit.
- Follow `docs/maths-policy.md`: every claim is checked by a test or visible on screen; colours are labelled artistic choices.
- Rendering is perturbation with rebasing: the reference orbit is computed in JS doubles (`referenceOrbit`) and read by the shader from a float texture (`uRef`, 4096 texels per row). Keep stage `iter` ≤ `REF_MAX` and stage spans above ~1e-13 or the picture breaks up.
- Escape-time pass 1 (`FS_ITER`, one source with `MANDEL`/`JULIA`/`SHIP` defines) runs only when the camera moves; pass 2 (`FS_COL`) runs every frame. Keep animation that does not change the geometry in pass 2. The Mandelbulb (`FS_BULB`) draws every frame; the Buddhabrot is drawn from images traced in advance (`data/buddha/`, below).
- Motion: `glide()` gives steady log-scale speed with eased ends. Escape-time views are computed in strips (`escapeCompute`, a few per frame, count adapted to frame time) for the camera where it will be (`predicted`), into a buffer 1.2× the screen; every frame shows the last finished view reprojected to the current camera (`uM`, `uOff` in `FS_COL`). Do not go back to computing the whole view per frame: that was the choppiness. Do not change render resolution mid-move (`adaptResolution`). `?film` uses `&strips=N` (default 1, no prediction); with N > 1 its exact clock aims each view precisely.
- Buddhabrot: traced in advance by `node tools/buddha-render.mjs` (all cores, ~20 min; `--quick` to try it) into `data/buddha/`: one 3072×1728 image per stage plus 2560×1440 images wherever a move would stretch the nearest image over 2× or leave the screen uncovered. The generator follows the page's camera path (`camera()` mirrors `camAt`'s Buddhabrot branch: keep them in step) and must be re-run after any change to Buddhabrot stages. Images store ln(path density) per colour on one scale per group of iteration limits; Metropolis-Hastings images are scaled to the larger image around them, so all blend without seams. The page (`FS_BUDDHA`, `chooseLayers`) lays up to three images per group coarse to fine through soft edges, with mipmaps; images load on demand, the next move's ahead of time (`buddhaPrefetch`), at most eight on the GPU. Exposure per stage comes from the generator and glides with the move. Never go back to live tracing: it cost grain, restarts and the processor cores the sound needed (it crackled).
- Pans use `towards()`, measured from the target's own span so they finish exactly on arrival (a pan measured from zero left shallow stages a few percent off: a visible jump at both ends of the move). Run `node tools/check-motion.mjs` (with `npm run serve`) after any change to the camera, timing or journey: it fails on jumps, sudden speed changes, and journeys that do not stop on every stage.
- The journey remembers which move it is on (`legFrom`); never work it out from `floor(T)`. With fixed steps (films, slow machines capped at 0.05 s) a move sums to 0.9999999999999999 and rounding sailed through the stage.
- Tour: **All** (`tour`) plays every set in turn; `nextInTour()` snapshots the canvas and `drawDissolve()` fades it over the next set's overview.
- Titles are coloured letter by letter (`letters(el, text, palette)`). Do not go back to a gradient with `background-clip: text`: Safari does not paint it through the animated letters, so titles vanish there.
- Recording: the Rec button is the main way to make films (Jakes records on his Mac, which renders in real time). It records `recCanvas`: the picture plus the stage text drawn by `recordTitles()`, at a fixed `recSize` (1920×1080 or vertical). From a standstill it films one journey as a finished piece (`recJourney.step`: intro card on black with sound rising, picture fades in, run, rest on the overview with no text and no tour hand-over (`final`, `recordQuiet()`), fade to black and silence, stop). Audio fades use `SND.fade()` on the audio clock. `tools/film.mjs` (virtual clock, frame by frame, `--set all`, `--strips`) and the `Film` workflow are for short clips only: software rendering takes 30+ s per deep 1080p frame. `tools/join.mjs` joins films with dissolves.
- Sound is tuned for meditation: soft pads, sparse singing-bowl bells, and a glissando about 26 dB under the pads. Measure loudness (rest vs a deep dive) before raising anything.
- Keep the sound engine light (it crackled at 6x real time): render it offline and keep it above 10x. The reverb is the main cost (mono in, 3.2 s tail); no audio-rate modulators on filter frequencies (they recompute the filter every sample: breathe from `update()` instead).
- Schedule sounds ahead of the audio clock, never at a time that may have passed (bells did, and jumped in with a click on busy frames). Stop voices on the audio clock after a ramp to exact zero, never from a page timer. The AudioContext asks for a 0.2 s buffer. Check for clicks by rendering offline and scanning the second difference of the samples.
- Auto resolution caps at ~2.1 million pixels; recording captures at 30 fps (`?recfps=24` for 24), 16 Mbit/s.
- New copies: `node tools/locate.mjs <re> <im>`; the test checks the true period (it divides the one the atom-domain search reports).
- To look at a stage, serve the folder (`npm run serve`) and screenshot `?set=<id>&at=<T>&scale=1&still` in headless Chrome with SwiftShader; `file://` cannot load the map.
